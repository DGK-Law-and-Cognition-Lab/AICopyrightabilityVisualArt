United States copyright protects only a person's contribution to a work built on AI-made material, so whoever applies it must judge the human part while knowing that the base was machine-made. We asked whether lay evaluators can. In five studies, \NPeople{} students rated AI-made images that a label attributed to an artist's design work or to his prompting of an AI system. On the same images, the AI label lowered creativity ratings by \SOneHOneCreatDiff{} points and effort ratings by \SOneHOneEffDiff{} on 1-to-5 scales (Study~1); stating that the artist had spent up to 100 hours did not close the gap. When he then edited each image by hand, ratings followed how much he changed it, and, compared across studies, the label carried much less into them: small and uneven, but not zero. An AI label acted more like information about who did what than like a stain on the work.
