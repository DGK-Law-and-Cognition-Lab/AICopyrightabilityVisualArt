====================================================================================================
STUDY 4 TEXT MEASURES: WHAT EACH RULE IS GROUNDED IN
====================================================================================================

One note per rule, naming the construct the rule measures, the reading of effort or
creativity it stands for, the literature that reading comes from, and what the rule
does and does not record. The notes are not part of the patterns: the patterns are in
text_rules.txt, and its checksum covers that file alone.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 1 OF 8: says_detail
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions visible detail, intricacy, complexity, texture, shading, brushwork, or quantity of content

Detail talk is the visible-work reading of effort: the marks on the picture surface from which a
viewer infers how much labour went in, since the labour itself cannot be seen. Buell and Norton
(2011) call the value people add to an identical result when they can see work being done on it
the labour illusion, and Kruger, Wirtz, Van Boven and Altermatt (2004) show that judged quality
follows believed effort most strongly when quality is hard to assess on its own, which a picture
from an unfamiliar maker is. The rule records the cue and not the inference: a writer who names
the detail is counted whether or not they went on to say it took work.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 2 OF 8: says_time
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions time, hours, duration, speed, or tediousness

Time talk is the elapsed-labour reading of effort: how long the making took, or how quickly it
could have been done. This is the cue Kruger, Wirtz, Van Boven and Altermatt (2004) manipulated
to show the effort heuristic, the rule of thumb by which a poem, a painting or a suit of armour
is rated better and worth more when it is said to have taken longer, and Chinander and
Schweitzer (2003) show the same reliance on time as an input measure even where it has no
bearing on the outcome being judged. Of the three effort readings this is the one closest to the
labour that Feist declined to protect, so the rule catches duration claims in either direction,
including the claim that something took no time at all.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 3 OF 8: says_skill
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions skill, craft, technique, talent, training, mastery, or hand execution

Skill talk is the execution reading of effort: ability acquired by training, and the craft with
which something was done. Amabile's componential model (1983, 1996) treats domain-relevant
skills, meaning technical skill and craft knowledge in a field, as one ingredient of creative
work and keeps them apart from the creativity-relevant skills that make the work new, and her
consensual assessment technique (1982) asks judges to rate creativity and technical goodness as
separate dimensions and finds them related but not the same. The rule records that a writer
located effort in ability rather than in time or in the picture surface; it does not record how
much ability they thought was shown.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 4 OF 8: says_concept
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions the idea, meaning, message, story, symbolism, or thought behind the work

Concept talk locates creativity in the idea behind the work rather than in the picture surface.
The construct is the lay implicit theory of creativity, meaning what ordinary people take the
word to consist of when nobody hands them a definition: Sternberg (1985) found that laypeople's
picture of a creative person turns on unconventional thinking and on imagination and aesthetic
sense, which he found absent from their picture of intelligence, and Runco and Bahleda (1986)
found that the picture differs by domain, so artistic creativity carries a profile of its own.
Leder, Belke, Oeberst and Augustin (2004) place interpretation, the viewer's attempt to make
sense of what a work is about, among the stages of taking in a work of art, which is why words
for meaning, message and story sit beside words for the idea. The rule counts a mention of the
idea; it does not judge whether the idea was any good.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 5 OF 8: says_prompt
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions prompting, typing, generating, or names a machine or image generator

Prompt talk is a written trace of what the participant believed about how the picture was made.
The instrument carries no manipulation check, meaning no question asking whether the participant
took in the vignette, and there is support for leaving one out: a check placed inside a study is
an intervention as well as a measure and can change what follows it (Hauser, Ellsworth and
Gonzalez 2018), and a question that asks whether a cover story was believed is itself a demand
characteristic, Orne's (1962) term for a cue that tells a participant what the study is about.
Chamberlain, Mullin, Scheerlinck and Wagemans (2018) found that viewers could not tell computer-
made pictures from hand-made ones and still rated pictures lower when told a computer made them,
so a participant told a person made these could plausibly doubt it. The rule records any mention
of a machine or of operating one, in either arm. It does not record what the writer believed
about who made the pictures.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 6 OF 8: prior_exposure
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: says the work looks familiar, already seen, generic or derivative (asserted), explicitly denies it (negated), or neither

Prior-exposure talk records a familiarity judgment: the claim that the work has been seen
before, is generic or typical, or the explicit denial of that. Familiarity is a criterion in its
own right in the literature on aesthetic preference, through mere exposure, the finding that
repeated encounters with a thing raise liking for it (Zajonc 1968), and through typicality, the
closeness of a thing to the usual example of its kind, which Hekkert, Snelders and van Wieringen
(2003) show works alongside novelty in shaping preference while pulling against it. On the
creativity side the standard definition requires originality as well as effectiveness (Runco and
Jaeger 2012), and Diedrich, Benedek, Jauk and Neubauer (2015) find that judged creativity leans
more on novelty than on usefulness, so a rater who reaches for newness is applying a criterion
the field itself uses. The rule takes three values because asserting familiarity and denying it
are different claims about the same thing.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 7 OF 8: anchor_echo
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: contains the rating scale's own wording

Anchor echo records an explanation that contains the rating scale's own labels. The motivation
is satisficing, Krosnick's (1991) term for giving an answer that is acceptable rather than the
best one can, of which the least effortful form for an open question is to hand back the words
already supplied, joined to Schwarz's (1999) observation that respondents read the wording of a
scale as a guide to what an acceptable answer looks like. The rule does not measure satisficing.
It fires on the scale's wording wherever the wording appears, including in an explanation that
goes on to give a full reason, so it records that the wording is present and nothing about what
else the explanation carries. It is a check on the corpus rather than one of the three
questions.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 8 OF 8: says_machine_made
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions a machine, a program or an image generator making the work, whether stated, denied or supposed

Machine-made talk records a mention of a machine making the work: a phrase such as 'AI
generated' or 'made by a computer', however it is used. The reading column beside it separates
statements that a machine made the work, hedged impressions such as 'probably AI' among them,
from denials ('this does not look AI generated') and suppositions ('if this were AI generated').
Neither the flag nor the statements measure disbelief in the vignette. The instrument has no
manipulation check, a participant can doubt the story without writing so, and a participant can
write that a picture looks machine-made while accepting what they were told about who made it;
the counts describe what was written.
