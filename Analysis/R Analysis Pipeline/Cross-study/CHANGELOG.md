## 2026-11-06 (evening), fixes from an independent review of the explanation measures

Two reviewers, one reading the code and one reading the paper's text against the tables, checked the three new measures. Changes:

- `label_from_meaning/LabelFromMeaning.py`:
  - **The test of the gain was wrong and is replaced.** The first version shuffled the labels, which also breaks the link between label and rating, so it tested whether the text predicts the label at all and its null was far too wide (95th percentiles 0.11 to 0.21). The new null keeps what meaning shares with the rating and length and shuffles only the rest (Freedman–Lane: residuals of each embedding dimension on rating and length, shuffled across participants), with the same folds and repeats as the real fit. With it, the gain from adding meaning is shown on effort in paper Studies 1 and 2 and on creativity in Study 5, where the old test showed none.
  - New check without the explanations that mention prompting or a machine (`says_prompt`). Without them, those three gains vanish (one other, Study 4 transformativeness, edges just past its null: +0.066 against +0.065).
  - New check with two other embedding models, `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5` and `mixedbread-ai/mxbai-embed-large-v1`, at pinned revisions, downloaded with the lead author's approval. `all-mpnet-base-v2` stays primary. Both newer models read slightly more from the text (meaning-only AUC on Study 1's effort explanations 0.81 and 0.83, against 0.80) and lead to the same conclusions: the gains on effort in Studies 1 and 2 and on Study 5's creativity clear their null with all three models; Study 4's essence change clears it with the two newer models and falls just short with the main one.
  - The embedding cache is keyed on the model, its revision, the library version and the texts, one file per model.
  - The README says that the AUC intervals hold each participant's prediction fixed and so are somewhat too narrow.
- `text_battery/origin_reason/`:
  - The rule is used for originals only. Scored by Part, it missed the pass marks for secondary works (agreement 82.0%, recall of no 74.0%), and the plan says a measure that fails is not used. `OriginReason_Analysis.R` now reports only where a Part meets the marks; the secondary-works shares are no longer called upper bounds.
  - The rating links now use the text battery's main model exactly: random intercepts for the artwork (not the artwork at each edit level), sum-to-zero contrasts, both label terms always in the model, Satterthwaite tests and intervals, and an AI-minus-Human contrast. Study 1's values are unchanged.
  - The shares table adds the AI-minus-Human difference and the AI-to-Human ratio, with intervals, which the plan named.
  - `OriginReason.py` now checks the codebook's checksum, the shared word-list module's checksum and the spaCy versions before scoring or applying; refuses a validation score that differs from the saved one; and writes `tables/origin_reason_apply_record.csv`, which the R step checks. Rescoring and reapplying reproduced the saved scores and codes exactly.
  - The READMEs describe the rule as it is built: spaCy splits sentences, and fixed patterns read them; there is no grammatical parse.
- `image_grounding/ImageGrounding_02_Analysis.R`: the models now hold every registered term (stimulus type in paper Study 1, image kind in Studies 3 and 5); the label effects barely change (Study 1 effort, −0.302 before and after). New version without the explanations that mention prompting or a machine: Study 1's effort difference shrinks from −0.30 to −0.18 standard deviations (95% interval −0.38 to 0.01). The README now says that length is CLIP tokens, at most 77.
- `beyond_word_lists/`: the R Markdown file and notebook show the new versions.

## 2026-11-06, text measures that use no word list

The lead author asked for analyses of the explanations beyond the fixed word lists. Three were added; all are exploratory, specified after every registered result was known, and change no registered verdict.

- `image_grounding/`: CLIP, a network trained to match pictures with captions, scores how closely each explanation describes its own picture against the other artworks (step 1 blind to label and rating; step 2 joins them). Validity: the explanation's own original is CLIP's closest picture in 42% and 38% of explanations of originals (chance 10%), 19% to 24% of explanations of Slight and Dramatic secondary works, and at chance at None. The edit-pull score (edited picture against its original) fails its validity check and is not used. AI-label effort explanations in Study 1 described the picture less (0.30 SD), also with the rating held constant; no label difference for secondary works.
- `label_from_meaning/`: whether the label can be predicted from sentence embeddings of the explanations beyond the rating and explanation length, per participant, mirroring the registered word-count recoverability check.
- `text_battery/origin_reason/`: a grammar rule that flags explanations giving how the work was made (by AI, by prompting, by a machine or by hand) as the reason for the rating. The lead author approved the codebook and plan the same day. The rule was adjusted on a development sample of 250 explanations, frozen (checksum in `origin_reason_freeze.md`), and scored once on a fresh validation sample of 400 against the majority of three model annotators: agreement 86.3%, recall 92.5% of yes and 80.8% of no, so it passed the marks fixed beforehand (85%, 75%, 75%). Scored by Part from the same codes, it met them for originals but not for secondary works, where it calls too many mentions reasons (precision 68.0%), so its shares there are upper bounds. `OriginReason.py apply` coded all 40,934 explanations; `OriginReason_Analysis.R` writes the shares by label, the within-label links to the rating and the by-Part validation table.
- `beyond_word_lists/`: `BeyondWordLists.ipynb` runs every Python step above, and `BeyondWordLists.Rmd` every R step, each calling the scripts in their own folders and showing the results.
- `label_from_meaning/LabelFromMeaning.py`: pins each process to one numerical thread and fits the 100 shuffled-label runs in parallel processes. The shuffles are drawn in the same order from the same generator, so the parallel run fits the same models as a sequential one. The first, sequential run was stopped after 90 minutes, before it wrote any table; the parallel run took 5.4 minutes on 12 cores.

## 2026-11-05, hours check for the paper's Study 2

Added `hours_check/`: `HoursCheck_Study2.R`, `README.md` and `tables/`. A co-author asked whether the small rise in creativity ratings of originals with stated hours (the paper's Study 2, the pipeline's Study 3) is reliable. The script refits Study 3's registered model and a refit with a random slope of stated time for each participant and of the label for each image. For each rating it writes 100 hours minus under 1 hour, averaged over the labels and within each label, and 10 hours minus under 1 hour. It stops unless the long data file has the checksum Study 3's run recorded and unless its refit reproduces the registered contrast. On creativity, 100 hours minus under 1 hour was 0.22 points under both models (95 percent interval 0.07 to 0.36 registered, 0.07 to 0.38 with slopes), the same under each label, and 10 hours did not differ from under 1 hour. The random-slope fit was singular. Exploratory; it changes no registered verdict.

## 2026-10-09

Added `CrossStudy_Penalty_Curve.Rmd`, `README.md` and this file. The analysis is exploratory. None of the five registrations contains a pooled model across studies; it was specified on 9 October 2026, after the registered results of Study 1 (7 October 2026) and Studies 2 to 5 (8 October 2026) were known, and nothing in it changes a registered verdict. The idea is the "propagation curve" of the program memorandum of 4 September 2026, Part IV.D.

- `CrossStudy_Penalty_Curve.Rmd` reads the five studies' deposited long data files and checks each against the run manifest its real run wrote, its `label_mode` column where it has one, and its registered counts. It checks that the three surveys of Studies 2, 4 and 5 show the same image files in the same questions. On each of the three measures it fits a pooled linear mixed model of Studies 2, 4 and 5, gives the Human minus AI penalty at each modification level pooled with equal study weights and within each study, the slope and curvature of the penalty across the levels, and the equivalence reading of the pooled penalty against plus or minus 0.224. It compares four nested models of the penalty (severance, taint, discounting and a free shape) by maximum likelihood, AIC, BIC and likelihood-ratio tests. It reads Study 1's registered creativity penalty on originals and recomputes Study 3's from its registered model. It draws two figures, copies them to `Docs/Writing/Final Paper/figures/`, and runs four checks: a cumulative-link model of creativity, each study left out in turn, the pooled model against each study's registered estimates, and the penalty at each time level of Studies 4 and 5.

Where the plan left a choice open, the code takes these readings.

- The time factors of Studies 4 and 5 are left out of the pooled model, so each study's penalty is averaged over them. The plan described these factors as having no registered effect. That holds for Study 4, where none of the six registered tests of source time survived Holm's correction, and for Study 5's editing-time effect, which survived on no measure. It does not hold for Study 5's source by editing-time interaction on creativity, which survived (Holm-corrected p = .048). The report says so, and section 10.4 gives the penalty at each time and level from each study's registered model.
- The four models of section 7 are written with numeric codes for the source, the study and the linear and bent shapes across the levels, so that each adds exactly three parameters to the one before. Written with factors, the discounting model drops columns that duplicate one another. The free-shape model equals the pooled model, and the run checks that their likelihoods agree.
- The participant and second-work intercepts of the cumulative-link model are entered as factors equal to `study:id` and `image:modification`.
- Study 1's 95 percent interval is computed from the standard error and degrees of freedom in its deposited table, which holds no interval.
- The equivalence reading uses Study 5's registered wording, with the uncorrected two-sided test as its significance half.

Run in place on 9 October 2026 with `Rscript -e 'rmarkdown::render("CrossStudy_Penalty_Curve.Rmd")'` from this folder; the knit ended cleanly in about a minute and a half.

- Every input check passed: each long data file matched its real run manifest, `label_mode` said `real` on every row of Studies 2, 4 and 5, and the counts matched the registrations (Study 1, 111 participants; Study 2, 235 and 20,659 analysed ratings; Study 3, 85; Study 4, 117 and 6,178; Study 5, 223 and 12,042 ratings, 11,768 analysed after the 274 six-point ratings). The 72 questions that display an image show the same files in the three surveys.
- Of 35 fits, 34 converged. Every pooled linear model and every leave-one-out model is singular, with the source-image variance at zero once each second work has its own intercept. The cumulative-link model of creativity did not converge under the rule: its optimizer returned code 0, but the update of the random effects inside it warned that it had not converged, and its largest absolute gradient was 0.0061. Its estimates are reported as description only.
- The recomputed Study 3 contrast reproduces Study 3's registered F test of source on creativity (t squared equals F). The refits of the registered models of Studies 4 and 5 reproduce each study's registered penalty at each level when averaged over time. The pooled model's within-study penalties differ from the registered ones by at most 0.004 rating points.
- The run manifest lists 22 outputs, all written by the run. No output names a folder particular to one machine.

The result, in brief. On creativity the pooled penalty on a second work is 0.01 rating points at None, 0.10 at Slight and 0.03 at Dramatic, each inside plus or minus 0.224; only Slight differs from zero. On originals it is 0.45 (Study 1) and 0.35 (Study 3). The penalty does not fall from None to Dramatic, which rules out the shape discounting predicts; it bends upward at Slight on all three measures. AIC favours the free shape and BIC favours severance on every measure. Without Study 4 the creativity penalty at Slight is 0.17 and its 90 percent interval passes the bound, and Study 5's creativity penalty comes mostly from its 10-hour editing-time cell. The report gives every number.

Not changed: the pipeline's top-level `README.md` does not yet list this folder.
