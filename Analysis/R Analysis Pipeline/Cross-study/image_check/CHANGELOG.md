### What was built

- `extract_stimuli.py` reads the six survey files (the final surveys of Studies 1 to 5 and the draft Study 5 survey) and the printout of the Study 5 survey, takes the embedded images out with poppler's `pdfimages -all` (poppler being a set of command-line tools for PDF files), and names each of the 30 stimulus images by its artwork, its version and its survey graphic ID. It requires three signals to agree (printout text and survey order, the sharing pattern of graphics and embedded images, and the pixel size the survey states), checks that each JPEG equals the bytes stored in the PDF file and each PNG holds the stored values exactly, checks the sRGB colour profile of every stimulus image, confirms that each None second work is the original's graphic, compares each image with the local files in `Stimuli/Images` when that folder is present, and writes `stimuli/` with the 30 images, `stimuli_manifest.csv`, `extraction_checks.csv` and, last, `extraction_run_manifest.csv`. That last file gives the size and SHA-256 checksum (a 64-character fingerprint of a file's exact bytes) of every input read and every file written, with the versions of poppler, Python, NumPy, pandas and Pillow. The script writes into a staging folder and moves the files into `stimuli/` only once every required check has passed.
- `ImageCheck.ipynb` checks every input against its recorded checksum, loads DINOv2 (`facebook/dinov2-base` at `f9e44c814b77203eaa57a6bdbbd535f21ede1415`) and CLIP (`openai/clip-vit-base-patch32` at `3d74acf9a28c67741b2f4f2ea7635f0aaf6f0268`) from the local model cache, checks each model file against the checksum it holds for it, computes each image's embedding (the list of numbers a network gives an image) with two inputs and its colour histogram (the shares of its pixels in a fixed grid of colours in CIELAB, a colour space in which equal steps look roughly equally different to the eye), writes the distance between every pair of the 30 images on five variants, applies the three checks, sets the distances beside the pooled mean ratings of Studies 2, 4 and 5, computes Spearman correlations (correlations computed on ranks), draws two figures, and writes the models, versions and run manifest.
- `requirements.txt` pins 81 packages: the 17 that the code imports or that run the notebook from the command line, and every package they depend on, each at the version installed in the Python 3.12.3 environment of the run.
- The one download, approved in chat on 10 October 2026: `config.json`, `preprocessor_config.json` and `model.safetensors` of `facebook/dinov2-base` at the pinned revision, 346,345,912 bytes of weights, Apache-2.0 licence. The weight file's SHA-256 checksum, `d73036b56966966d07975d696bde331762f37297e2f095de8cea0040c3aa0841`, equals the one the model repository lists. CLIP was already in the local cache at its pinned revision. Nothing was installed.

### Readings taken where the specification left a choice open

- **The survey of record.** The final Study 5 survey is the authority for which graphic each block shows. The draft Study 5 survey and the final surveys of Studies 2 and 4 must show the same graphic in every question of every stimulus block, with the same stated sizes and the same rating items, and they do. The graphic of a question is read from its displayed text (`QuestionText`). The stored copy `QuestionText_Unsafe` names the Slight graphic in all ten None second-work questions of each derivative survey; the printout shows the original there, and the extraction records the discrepancy and checks the printout against it.
- **The printout's survey.** The displayed wording of the draft and the final Study 5 survey differs in three questions, QID163, QID164 and QID17, once spaces before punctuation are set aside (a fourth question, QID236, differs only in such a space and in italics). The printout carries the draft's wording at all three. The script checks that these are the only three questions that differ, and that each of the six phrases lies in exactly one survey. The printout is therefore a print of the draft, or of the final survey before those edits, since the final was made as a copy of the draft; the two show the same graphics.
- **The three signals.** Signal 1 pairs the survey's 70 displays, laid out in the printout's block order, with the printout's 70 stimulus images in page order; each image must lie between the page of its printed label (or, for an artwork shown alone, its block name) and the page of the next printed label or block name. Signal 2 requires one embedded image per survey graphic and one graphic per embedded image. Signal 3 compares the size stated in the survey's page code with the image's pixel size; the survey states it for 29 of 30 graphics. The local files are a fourth, recorded signal that decides nothing.
- **Images that are not stimuli.** The two images printed before the first stimulus block, on page 1 (1501 by 705 pixels) and page 3 (304 by 79 pixels), are left out.
- **File names and formats.** Files are named `<artwork>_<version>` with the version in lower case, and the extension follows the stored format: `.png` for an image stored with lossless compression and `.jpg` for one stored as JPEG. Nine of the Slight versions are therefore `.png` and `1NonFigure`'s is `.jpg`. Of an image printed more than once, the first printing is kept; every printing of one embedded image decodes to the same pixels.
- **The pixel checksum.** The SHA-256 of the text `<width>x<height>x3;` followed by the decoded 8-bit red, green and blue values, row by row.
- **The local cross-check.** Each stimulus is compared with every local image of the same size, including the images inside local PDF files, which `pdfimages` opens; a PDF's transparency masks, which `pdfimages -list` marks as `smask` or `mask`, are left out. Closeness is the mean absolute difference of the red, green and blue values on the 0 to 255 scale, with the largest single difference, the number of pixels that differ by more than 40 in some channel, and the box of rows and columns that holds them. A second comparison scales the stimulus and every local image whose ratio of width to height lies within 2 percent of its own to 256 pixels wide, with the Lanczos filter (a resampling filter that weighs nearby pixels by a smooth curve), and records the closest. Without the local folder, the cross-check is skipped and recorded as skipped.
- **Paths.** Every path printed or written is relative to the project root. A file that an environment variable places outside it is written relative to that folder, with the folder's label in angle brackets.
- **The primary network input.** Each image is resized to 224 by 224 pixels with Pillow's bicubic filter, the filter both networks' own preprocessing uses, and the network's processor then only rescales the values to 0 to 1 and normalises them by the network's own means and spreads. The robustness input is the processor's standard path unchanged.
- **The embeddings.** DINOv2's is `pooler_output`, the class token (the one slot that summarises the whole image) after the final layer normalisation, 768 numbers; CLIP's is `get_image_features`, 512 numbers. Each image goes through the network alone, in float32 (32-bit floating point numbers), on one thread for intra-operation and inter-operation work, with PyTorch's deterministic setting on. The embeddings are converted to 64-bit numbers before the cosine arithmetic, and a cosine distance that rounding leaves below 0 is set to 0.
- **The colour measure.** The box filter (each new pixel the plain average of the old pixels it covers), rounding the new size to the nearest whole pixel; the standard sRGB transfer function (the curve that turns a stored value into an amount of light) and the sRGB to XYZ matrix (XYZ being the colour coordinates from which CIELAB is computed); the white point taken as the XYZ value of sRGB white (0.95047, 1.0000001, 1.08883), the D65 white (the standard daylight white of sRGB) to the matrix's precision, so that white maps to L 100, a 0 and b 0 with no rounding past the range; the CIELAB function with the threshold (6/29) cubed. The fixed ranges are L 0 to 100, a -87 to 99 and b -108 to 95, the smallest whole-number ranges that hold every sRGB colour (the extremes are a -86.18 and 98.23, b -107.86 and 94.48). The notebook checks the ranges on a grid of 52 levels per channel and checks that no pixel of any image falls outside them. The Hellinger distance is the square root of 1 minus the sum over cells of the square root of the product of the two shares.
- **The None distance.** The None second work has no file of its own. Its distance is computed between the original and a second, separate pass of the original through each network or histogram, which must give the same numbers bit for bit, and must be below 5 x 10^-7.
- **Pass rules for checks 2 and 3.** Check 2 passes on a variant when all ten artworks have the Slight version closer than the Dramatic version; check 3 when all 20 edited images rank their own original first. An original tied with the image's own original counts as a failure.
- **Which measure moves most at Slight.** The median, over the ten artworks, of the ratio of the Slight distance to the Dramatic distance.
- **The ratings.** Five-point items only, read with every value as text and `keep_default_na=False`. Each rating item is linked to an image through the manifest's list of the items asked about it, and the run checks the image, level and question columns against that link. Within a study, the mean over all participants is the mean of every rating of that image and question, whatever the arm (the AI or Human label group); the pooled mean is the mean of the three study means and is left empty unless all three studies have the cell. Transformativeness of `1NonFigure` at None and at Slight therefore has no pooled mean, and its correlations run over 19 and 9 images.
- **The correlations.** Computed for all five variants, all three questions, over all participants and in each arm, across the 20 edited images and within each level; the README reports the primary input over all participants.
- **The figures.** Figure 1 uses the primary input; figure 2 uses the primary input and the pooled means over all participants. Each carries the full status in two lines at its foot. The PDF files are written without a creation time.
- **Versions.** The notebook stops if the Python version or any analysis package differs from `requirements.txt`, ignoring a build label such as `+cpu`, unless `IMAGE_CHECK_ALLOW_OTHER_VERSIONS=1` is set. It reads the networks only from the local cache, and the README gives the one command that fetches them. The project root falls back to the pipeline folder when the folders are laid out differently. The notebook names the `python312` kernel, as the pipeline's other notebooks do. Tables give six decimal places.
