# Structural topics changelog

The edit history of the source file in this folder. The source file carries none itself.

## 2026-10-09

Added `StructuralTopics.Rmd`, `README.md` and this file. The analysis is exploratory: it was specified on 9 October 2026, after every registered result of the five studies was known, no registration names it, and it changes no registered verdict. The justifications were written after the ratings, so nothing in it is evidence of why anyone rated as they did.

- `StructuralTopics.Rmd` reads the long data files, word counts and item-scale tables of Studies 2, 4 and 5, and checks each against the record of the real run that wrote it, `label_mode`, and the registered counts. It reads the function words, the task vocabulary and the word pattern from `shared/python/study_text.py`, and checks that the pattern, applied in R, reproduces every study's deposited word counts. It removes the task vocabulary with its plurals and each study's own item words, keeps the words used in at least 5 responses of each question's pooled corpus, and drops the responses left with no word. For each question it chooses the number of topics with `stm::searchK` over K = 4 to 12 by the highest held-out likelihood, fits a structural topic model with prevalence `~ source * modification + rating_c + study` from the spectral start with seed 20260421, refits it from the LDA start with seeds 20260421 to 20260425, and estimates the label's effect on each topic with `stm::estimateEffect` and "Global" uncertainty: the AI minus Human difference at each level, averaged over levels, and the change from None to Dramatic, the last two with the Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment as description. It draws one greyscale figure per question and records fit status and a run manifest with `shared/R/fit_status.R`.

Where the specification left a choice open, the code takes these readings.

- Each study's rule removes that study's own item words, so the 14 words that Studies 4 and 5 put in their creativity and essence definitions (*contribution*, *core*, *creation*, *defined*, *feeling*, *intellectual*, *least*, *makes*, *observing*, *original*, *presence*, *refers*, *sense*, *unique*) are removed from their responses and kept in Study 2's, and *consider*, from a six-point item stem of Study 2, is removed from Study 2's responses only.
- The task vocabulary is removed with the plural of each task word, as the shared module removes it; item words are removed as written.
- The centred rating is centred on the mean rating of each question's modelled responses.
- The stability check pairs each spectral topic with its best match in each refit, not one to one, and counts the refits in which two spectral topics share a best match.
- The effects are computed from 500 draws from each of the 25 coefficient sets of `estimateEffect`, as stm's own summary of the effects does; each interval is the mean of the draws plus or minus the t quantile times their standard deviation.
- The pipeline's rule for a model that did not converge, written in `shared/R/fit_status.R` for the mixed models, is applied to the topic models too: such a model's estimates are kept as description, and its standard errors, intervals, t statistics and p-values are set to missing with a note.

Run in place on 9 October 2026 with `Rscript -e 'rmarkdown::render("StructuralTopics.Rmd")'` from this folder. A first knit ran to its end in about 42 minutes. It showed that the essence change model did not converge within 500 iterations, and the convergence rule above, the last relative change of each fit's bound, a generated reading of the held-out likelihood across K, and corrected cross-references in section 2.2 were added before the final knit, which also ran to its end in about 42 minutes. Every number the two knits share agrees.

- All 42 input checks passed. Each long data file and item-scale table matched its real run's record, each word-count file matched its text run manifest, `label_mode` said `real` on every row, and the counts matched the registrations (Study 2, 235 participants and 20,659 analysed ratings; Study 4, 117 and 6,178, with 140 six-point ratings left out; Study 5, 223 and 11,768, with 274 left out). The R reading of the shared word pattern reproduced the deposited word counts of all three studies exactly.
- The rule removes 22 task words and 8 plurals of task words from every study, 22 item words from Study 2's responses and 35 from those of Studies 4 and 5. The rarity rule then removes 1,421 words from the creativity corpus, 1,554 from transformativeness and 1,510 from essence change.
- Creativity: of 13,163 analysed responses, 1,876 kept no word and were dropped, leaving 11,287 (5,770 Human, 5,517 AI) from 575 participants, with a vocabulary of 1,018 words and 39,924 word tokens, 3.54 per response on average. Transformativeness: 12,279, 1,585 dropped, 10,694 modelled, 1,080 words, 3.91 per response. Essence change: 13,163, 1,878 dropped, 11,285 modelled, 1,118 words, 3.69 per response.
- The chosen K is 8 for creativity (held-out likelihood -5.5295, against -5.5353 at the runner-up, K = 6), 10 for transformativeness (-5.4751, against -5.4788 at K = 5) and 11 for essence change (-5.4247, against -5.4320 at K = 7). The held-out likelihood does not rise or fall steadily with K on any question, and the differences that decide the choice are small. Eight of the 27 searchK fits reached the limit of 500 iterations, among them the fits at the chosen K of creativity and essence change.
- The creativity model converged after 405 iterations and the transformativeness model after 273. The essence change model did not converge within 500 iterations: at the last iteration its bound still rose by 0.0000187 of itself, against the threshold of 0.00001. Its label effects are therefore description only, with no interval or p-value. All 15 LDA refits converged.
- Stability is uneven. The mean best-match correlation of a topic with the five LDA refits ranges from 0.151 to 0.943 on creativity (median 0.451), from 0.116 to 0.872 on transformativeness (median 0.628) and from 0.175 to 0.910 on essence change (median 0.410). On creativity only topics 6, 4 and 3 reach 0.697 or more.
- Creativity, the primary question. Averaged over levels, the AI minus Human difference has a Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-value below .05 for all 8 topics, and its change from None to Dramatic for 6 of 8; 15 of the 24 differences at single levels have a 95 percent interval that excludes zero. The largest averaged difference and the largest change both belong to topic 7, whose highest-probability words are *ai, similar, feel, takes, painting, generated, making*: AI-arm responses gave it 3.8 percentage points more of their expected share at None, 1.7 more at Slight and 0.2 fewer at Dramatic, a change of -4.1 points (95 percent interval -4.8 to -3.3). Its mean best-match correlation is 0.389, so another start does not recover it well. Topic 4 (*difference, look, looks, see, changes, identical, exact*), which is stable (0.848), took 3.5 points less of the AI arm's expected share at None (95 percent interval -4.7 to -2.2); at Slight and at Dramatic its interval includes zero.
- Transformativeness. Averaged over levels, 5 of 10 topics have an adjusted p-value below .05, and 5 of 10 for the change; 16 of the 30 differences at single levels have an interval that excludes zero. Topic 10 (*look, transformation, identical, differences, added, basically, photos*) took 8.6 points more of the AI arm's expected share at None, 3.2 more at Slight and 1.1 more at Dramatic, and topic 4 (*difference, see, looks, changes, exact, exactly, looked*) took 7.2 points less at None. Both topics have *identical* or *exact* among their seven highest-probability words, so at None the label shifted share between two topics whose top words overlap. Their mean best-match correlations are 0.640 and 0.822.
- Essence change, description only. The largest averaged difference is that of topic 1 (*overall, due, photo, elements, focus, remained, drawing*), -0.9 percentage points, and the largest change from None to Dramatic that of topic 4 (*difference, look, see, changes, looks, identical, exact*), 2.0 points.
- Every interval treats the responses of one participant as independent, so the intervals are likely too narrow and the counts of adjusted p-values below .05 likely too high.
- An independent check refitted the creativity model and computed the label differences by stm's own route (`plot.estimateEffect` with a moderator, from fresh draws). Every difference at every level agreed with the table to within 0.0002 of expected share, the difference at None agreed with the AI coefficient of stm's summary and the change with the AI by Dramatic coefficient to within 0.0001, and the topic words were the same. A recount of the three corpora with the shared Python module itself gave the same responses, vocabularies and word tokens.
- The run manifest lists 20 outputs, all written by the run. No output names a folder particular to one machine.
