====================================================================================================
STUDY 1 TEXT MEASURES: CANDIDATE WORDS FOR EACH RULE
====================================================================================================

WHAT THIS FILE SHOWS

Each rule below is followed by up to 30 words. They are words over-represented in
the responses that rule flags, compared with the rest of the corpus, and not named
by the rule itself: the words that behave like the rule's own words without being
on its list.

The list is reported so that a reader can judge the rules' coverage. A word here
that plainly names a rule's topic shows a hole in that rule's vocabulary; a word that
merely sits beside the rule's subject shows nothing of the kind, and most words are
expected to be of that second kind. No word is ever added to a rule after the data
have been seen: the rules stand as written in text_rules.txt, whatever this list
holds, and nothing in the notebook reads this file back.

The example under each word is a real response containing it, taken from the
responses the rule does NOT flag, so it shows the response the rule would
have caught had the word been on its list. Where a word appears only inside
already-flagged responses, the example is labelled as such.

WHAT THIS FILE IS NOT. It does not measure recall, the share of its subject a rule
catches. Measuring that would need a person to read responses the rule stayed silent
on and judge them one at a time, because a response can be a miss without containing
any unusual word: it can say the thing plainly, in common words. This file shows
phrasings a rule may have missed. It produces no percentage, no confidence interval
and no denominator, and none should be reported from it.

Words are ranked by the log-odds score described in section 8 of the notebook. A
higher score means more concentrated in the flagged responses. Words appearing in
fewer than 5 responses are not listed.

Also not listed, whatever their score: alex, art, artwork, image, picture, piece, work,
effort, effortful, creative, creativity, rating, rate, involved, production, much,
minimal, moderate, significant, great, deal, lot, and their plurals. These words are the
task's own (the vignette's name, the words of the two rating questions and their scale
anchors, and the words describing the artworks) and cannot name any rule's topic, so
they are not listed.
