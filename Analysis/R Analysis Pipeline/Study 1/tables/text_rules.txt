====================================================================================================
STUDY 1 TEXT MEASURES: THE RULES IN FULL
====================================================================================================

Every measure below is applied by computer to all 2,220 responses, identically,
with the response text lower-cased first. No response is judged individually.
Patterns are written in verbose mode: spaces and line breaks inside a pattern are
ignored by the matcher, so a literal space is written as \  or \s.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULE 1 OF 8: says_detail
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In plain words: mentions visible detail, intricacy, complexity, texture, shading, brushwork, or quantity of content

COUNTS AS A MATCH:
    (?x)
    \b detail (?:s|ed|ing)? \b

    | \b complex (?:ity)? \b | \b complicated \b
    | \b elaborate \b
    | \b textur (?:e|es|ed|al) \b
    | \b shading \b | \b shadow (?:s|ing)? \b | \b hatching \b
    | \b brush \s? (?: work | stroke s? ) \b | \b stroke s? \b
    | \b line \s? work \b | \b fine \s lines? \b
    | \b meticulous (?:ly)? \b | \b precise (?:ly)? \b | \b precision \b
    | \b layer (?:s|ed|ing)? \b
    | \b (?: a\ lot | lots | so\ much | too\ much | much | plenty | a\ great\ deal )

THE NEGATION WINDOW (used by prior_exposure AND by the four polarity readings:
detail_polarity, time_polarity, skill_polarity, concept_polarity)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The window is the 30 characters immediately before a matched phrase. For a
prior-exposure phrase, a negating word from the first list below inside that
window means the response is recorded as 'negated' rather than 'asserted'. For a
detail, time, skill or concept phrase, the same window is searched for the same
negating words and also for the quantifier negations in the second list, which
name a small amount rather than deny a fact. Thirty characters is about five or
six words: long enough to reach back over 'I do not think I have', short enough not to
reach far into the sentence.

Negating words, used by prior_exposure and by all four polarity readings:
    (?x)
    \b not \b | \b never \b | n't \b | \b no \b | \b nothing \b | \b none \b
    | \b hardly \b | \b rarely \b | \b unlike \b | \b without \b
    | \b cannot \b | \b can't \b | \b barely \b | \b nowhere \b

Quantifier negations, used by the four polarity readings only, never by
prior_exposure ('little' or 'low' before 'familiar' is not a denial of familiarity):
    (?x)
    \b little \b | \b not \s much \b | \b not \s a \s lot \b | \b not \s many \b
    | \b lack \b | \b lacking \b | \b lacks \b
    | \b no \b | \b minimal \b | \b barely \b | \b hardly \b | \b without \b | \b low \b

'little' is a quantifier negation only where it names an amount ('little detail',
'very little', 'too little', 'little to no'). It is not read as one when the words
directly before it match this pattern, which marks it as a size ('a little', 'the
little details', 'many small little parts'):
    (?x)
    \b (?: a | the | some | such | many | so \s many | a \s lot \s of | lots \s of
    | all \s the | small | tiny ) \s+ $

A negating word counts only if it sits in the same clause as the phrase it
negates. The window is therefore cut at the nearest of these clause markers,
and only the text after the last one is searched for a negating word:
    (?x)
    , | ; | \. | \b because \b | \b but \b | \b however \b | \b since \b
    | \b although \b | \b though \b | \b whereas \b | \b so \s that \b

For prior_exposure, a negating word from the first list sitting inside the matched
phrase itself ('have never seen', 'haven't seen') also records it as 'negated', and
so does a phrase that carries its own negation in its spelling:
    (?x) ^ (?: unfamiliar | never | un [-] )
Two phrases assert familiarity through a negating word and are recorded as
'asserted' before either reading is applied:
    (?x) ^ (?: nothing | not ) \s new $

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE POLARITY READING (says_detail, says_time, says_skill, says_concept)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each of these four flags records a topic, whatever the writer said about it. A
separate polarity column beside each flag records whether the matched phrase was
claimed or denied: 'negated' if a negating word or a quantifier negation from the
two lists under THE NEGATION WINDOW above sits in the same clause-cut window
before the phrase or begins the phrase itself; 'asserted' otherwise; 'neither'
when the flag is false. The quantifier negations deny an amount rather than a
fact, which is why they are kept apart from the negating words and are not used
for prior-exposure talk.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MACHINE-MADE READING (machine_made_reading, beside says_machine_made)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
says_machine_made records a phrase about a machine making the work, however it is
used. machine_made_reading reads every such phrase in the response, outside the
rule's exclusions. A phrase is not asserted if a negating word from the first list
under THE NEGATION WINDOW sits inside it or in the same clause-cut window before it,
or if a word that opens a supposition, from the list below, sits in that window;
otherwise it is asserted, hedged impressions ('probably AI', 'looks AI generated')
included. The response is 'asserted' if any of its phrases is asserted,
'not_asserted' if it has phrases and none is asserted, and 'neither' exactly when
says_machine_made is false.

Words that open a supposition ('as if' does not count):
    (?x)
    (?<! \b as \s ) \b if \b | \b whether \b | \b unless \b
